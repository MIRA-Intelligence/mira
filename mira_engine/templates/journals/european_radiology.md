## Official requirements

### Article-specific content — article-types page

| Component | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Main text / introduction | ≤3,000 / 400 words |
| Abstract | ≤250 words: Objectives; Materials and Methods; Results; Conclusion |
| Figures / tables | ≤6 / 5 |
| Key Points | Question 20–25 words; Findings 20–25; Clinical Relevance Statement ≤40 |
| Title | ≤15 words recommended, not a hard ceiling |
| Keywords | Article page: 1–5 |

Methods must state ethics/consent, study design and dates, selection, reference
standard, procedures and statistics. Results open with the final cohort and use
Figure 1 for participant flow. Discussion covers interpretation, comparison,
limitations and a supported conclusion. Select study-appropriate reporting guidance;
do not apply systematic-review allowances to a primary study.

### File and reference requirements — mandatory-files page

Provide separate title page, main document and Disclosure Paragraph. Title page:
authors, affiliations and corresponding contacts; no running title. Number
manuscript pages. The submission does not accept LaTeX sources or PDF manuscripts;
prepare Word. Use consistent English spelling and define uncommon abbreviations.
References use sequential square-bracket numbers and must be published or accepted,
with a DOI available. Complete the official disclosure form; it is a review gate,
not an optional acknowledgment. Identify relevant editorial-board involvement.
This page requests 3–5 keywords, unlike the article page.

### Figures, tables and graphical abstract — artwork page

Use self-contained captions, defined symbols/units and clear panel labels. Table
footnotes explain abbreviations and formatting; avoid colored/highlighted cells.
Supplementary material accompanies initial submission and is reviewed; accepted
supplements are published without reformatting. The graphical abstract is mandatory
at revision for Original Articles; use the publisher's linked template. The overview
table calls it mandatory without stating the stage.

### Review and revisions — general-information page

Review is single-blinded: do not enforce anonymous author manuscripts. All authors
must be listed, and submission must be performed by an author. Revisions require
clean and annotated versions; follow the decision letter for responses. A rejection
without resubmission invitation cannot be treated as permission to resubmit.
Disclose and cite preprints. Joint authorship and author-list changes need attention
to the current policy rather than assumptions borrowed from other journals.

## MIRA execution and clarification (not additional publisher rules)

1. Clarify primary research versus systematic review, article stage and selected
   experiment when not already known. Use request_workflow_interaction for material
   conflicts; an approval form does not waive a publisher's rule.
2. Default to 3–5 keywords, which satisfies both published ranges; record that this
   is MIRA's reconciliation, not a new journal rule. If the author requests fewer,
   flag the disagreement before finalizing.
3. Track graphical-abstract readiness as a stage-specific task. Prepare a draft
   early if authorized, but do not report its absence as an established initial-
   submission rejection condition. If the live portal demands one at initial
   submission, surface that discrepancy and obtain guidance.
4. Build the narrative from supplied cohort and experiment evidence. Reconcile
   patient, image and lesion counts across source tables before writing. Treat
   unavailable exclusions, approvals or analyses as missing facts, not boilerplate.
5. Read the current linked Disclosure Paragraph template or ask for it when needed.
   Do not label a generic conflicts paragraph as the completed official form.
   Author confirmation is needed for signatures, funding, consent, conflicts,
   contributor identity and AI assistance.
6. Keep original figure data and inspect exports for legibility, privacy and
   consistency. Do not fabricate a flowchart count or a patient-benefit claim.
   Bundle manuscript and associated forms/graphics as ONE delivery candidate.

## Editorial recommendations (MIRA)

Explain a concrete radiological question and its relevance to a patient-facing
decision. Describe recruitment, reference standard, clinical comparators and reader
procedures. Prefer a coherent quantitative result with uncertainty to a list of
significant associations. Discuss transport across scanners, centers and populations
only to the extent examined. Distinguish improved image quality from better diagnosis,
and better diagnosis from improved treatment outcomes.

## Preflight and unresolved checks (MIRA)

Measure all relevant counts and confirm the export rather than inferring compliance
from its extension. The keyword ranges and graphical-abstract stage difference are
explicit source discrepancies; do not hide them. The overview lists no fixed
reference-count ceiling, but that is not permission for irrelevant citations.
This snapshot does not reproduce all linked Springer AI/artwork policies or the
current disclosure form: final compliance requires those checks where applicable.
Record missing source access, author answers or formatting verification in the
compliance note; never declare full submission readiness while such gaps remain.
