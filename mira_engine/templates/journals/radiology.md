## Official requirements

### Article limits and structure — author guide

| Component | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Main text | ≤3,000 words, Introduction through Discussion |
| Sections | Introduction; Materials and Methods; Results; Discussion |
| Section ceilings | Introduction 400; Results 1,000; Discussion 800 words |
| Abstract | ≤300 words; Background; Purpose; Materials and Methods; Results; Conclusion |
| References / figures / tables | ≤35 / 6 / 4 |
| Key Results | ≤3 items; ≤75 words combined; numerical findings |
| Summary | One sentence, ≤30 words |
| Title | ≤15 words |
| Human-study displays | Demographics in Table 1; participant flow in Figure 1 |
| Review | Double-anonymized |
| Text source | DOCX; January 2026 update excludes DOC |

Retrospective studies must not acquire a purported prospective hypothesis during
writing. Keep author/institution/funding identifiers out of the reviewer manuscript.

### Submission package — preparation checklist

Provide a separate identifying title page and cover letter, an anonymous manuscript,
and the applicable human-study checklist. The cover letter addresses exclusive
submission, conflicts and cohort overlap. Initial figures go into one separate
Word document, ordered with adjacent captions; revision figures are separate.
Combine supplementary text/tables/figures into another Word file; upload multimedia
separately. The abstract must identify design, dates, groups and statistical methods,
then numerical outcomes with denominators and appropriate uncertainty. Check
consistency between the abstract, tables and body; distinguish inapplicable checklist
items from unanswered ones.

### Reporting and displays — scientific style guide

Describe the index test and reference standard distinctly. Identify prespecified
outcomes, acquisition details and evaluation procedures. Define uncommon
abbreviations independently in abstract and body; the abbreviation list has at most
10 entries. Use interpretable axes, units and captions, and a labelled color scale
when color encodes values. MRI captions need sequence information not already
adequately supplied in Methods. Survival plots need censoring marks and numbers
at risk. Discuss limitations immediately before the concluding paragraph. Do not
introduce previously unreported analyses in Discussion.

### Declarations — RSNA policies

Disclose AI writing/image assistance in both manuscript and cover letter, naming
the tool/version, use and relevant details. Humans remain responsible.
The full title page needs a truthful data-sharing statement, including restrictions.
Trial registration and human/animal ethics statements must reflect actual approvals.

## MIRA execution and clarification (not additional publisher rules)

1. Build an evidence ledger before drafting: requested claim → project result →
   cohort/analysis version → intended paragraph or figure. Missing source evidence
   stays TODO, not a plausible fabricated number.
2. Agree the clinical question and selected result with the author. Use
   request_workflow_interaction when the available experiment supports a different
   claim, article type or target than the requested deliverable.
3. Collect identity-bearing material separately. Scan the review copy's text,
   document properties, captions, embedded screenshots and file names for leaks.
   Do not erase the original evidence or change the scientific description.
4. Ask together for absent consent/ethics details, trial information, contributor
   identities, funding, competing interests and related manuscripts. Do not infer
   a waiver, exclusive submission or permission to reuse a figure.
5. Treat technical formatting as work to perform, not a claim implied by a filename.
   Reopen the exported document; examine headings, references, equations, tables,
   symbols and figures. Report any renderer or font limitations.
6. Package the approved manuscript and its associated files as ONE delivery
   candidate. Maintain a file manifest distinguishing present, draft, missing,
   not applicable and author-confirmation-required items.

## Editorial recommendations (MIRA)

Lead with a specific clinical decision or diagnostic uncertainty. Explain how
recruitment, the reference standard and reader procedures support that decision.
Keep algorithm internals subordinate to clinical interpretation. Prefer an explicit
effect and uncertainty over an isolated best score. Discuss selection bias,
transportability and practical limitations. Do not turn retrospective accuracy
into improved patient outcomes. A concise working draft with acknowledged gaps is
more defensible than unsupported submission-readiness.

## Preflight and unresolved checks (MIRA)

Measure all limits above and report the counting scope. Check that every substantive
claim has evidence, every cited work exists, and each callout resolves. Produce a
compliance note linking the exact source section used. A preparation checklist is
not a substitute for the Materials and Methods section even if its overview list
omits that heading. Do not infer an independent graphical-abstract mandate or
transfer another journal's supporting-file rules. Check current publisher
instructions and any decision letter before actual submission; unresolved conflicts
trigger clarification, not silent precedence chosen to avoid a requirement.
